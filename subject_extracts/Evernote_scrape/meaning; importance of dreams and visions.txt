



The dream state, according to the divine and expressly revealed precepts, cannot be equated or compared. Lay aside the dream and seize hold of that which the All-Bountiful hath revealed in His Book. AvK1.322, PA00654 (provisional translation)




Dreams are of three kinds. One kind is idle fancy, and the dream of the heedless. Whatever is spoken and heard during the day is seen in the world of dreams during the night. Another kind consisteth of spiritual discovery, which is clouded in fancy. Such dreams need interpretation.... The third type of dreams are such as have true significance, and these are pure spiritual discoveries. (From a Tablet of 'Abdu'l-Baha, in a compilation on dreams) TC00180

Dreams are of three distinct categories. The first comprises true visions, which, like the radiance of dawn, require no interpretation. That which is beheld manifests precisely as witnessed, though this phenomenon rarely, if indeed ever, graces the majority of humankind throughout their earthly existence. Such dreams descend upon an individual when the heart is unburdened and the faculty of memory is cleansed of all vain thoughts. That which the spirit discovers is reflected with pristine clarity - these are true visions, requiring no interpretation and manifesting in precise accordance with reality. The second category encompasses dreams requiring interpretation. In such instances, the heart and mind harbor idle thoughts, and while the soul undertakes a spiritual journey, interpretation becomes necessary. One must separate the vain thoughts from the spiritual revelations. It is akin to a white fabric that readily accepts any hue applied to it - this represents reality. However, should that fabric already possess a color and another be applied, the result deviates from reality. For instance, if yellow resides in the fabric and blue is applied, green emerges. Thus, one must separate the yellow to allow the blue to manifest - this constitutes interpretation. The third category consists of confused dreams, comparable to when one becomes embroiled in conflict during the day, and these events are reflected in the realm of sleep. Such dreams are mere confused visions, bearing neither interpretation nor revelation. Prior to slumber, delusive thoughts had dominated the individual's consciousness. These manifestly require no interpretation, being merely confused dreams. In the estimation of the Prophets, dreams constitute a form of divine revelation, particularly among the Israelite Prophets, for whom the phenomenon of dreams held profound significance. Some Prophets received their divine mission during nocturnal visions, wherein they beheld a luminous personage or divine form addressing them, commanding: "Arise, proceed to the land of Israel, and proclaim thus and so." Upon awakening, they would arise and pronounce: "Regarding that matter which perplexes you, this is its nature," or "Its resolution lies herein." This manifestation occurs independently of human will, though one's preparedness facilitates its emergence. It resembles a guest's arrival - while they shall come regardless, their entry is facilitated when the host has swept and prepared the dwelling. (Abdu'l-Baha, ABU0059, provisional translation [Claude 3.5s], AvK1.325-327)
