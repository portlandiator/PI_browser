# Ascension of Baha'u'llah

Bahá’u’lláh’s Ascension marks His passing from earthly life and the profound grief of those who loved Him. The community mourned separation while affirming that His spiritual influence and the Cause continued after His departure. For ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá and the believers, bereavement carried an urgent call to remain steadfast, teach, and serve rather than seek ease.

[Bahá’u’lláh](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=3491B0317F6A4084995CD331AD2C278E)’s life and passing anchor the subject; the linked writings on [suffering and imprisonment](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=370AC40D2874E1246B99EB8122E23C9A) recall the afflictions that preceded His death. [Tests and trials](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=CC093CE37A394C1F81BEAF67251D3FC4) extends the theme of endurance into the grief and upheaval that followed. [Teaching the Cause](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=48991B831FEB414988B143D181EB0DEF) and [Covenant-breaking](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=42BA11FC5FE24CBEA769298634A7FA42) point to the responsibilities and disputes that arose after His Ascension: service and proclamation continued, while challenges to the Covenant tested the community’s unity. The [grief-and-lamentation](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=8B74AA4BF06FE197BC2DFCF83BE17C14) link gives direct voice to sorrow at separation. These are related responses to the same loss, not substitutes for the historical event itself.
