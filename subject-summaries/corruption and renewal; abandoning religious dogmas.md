# corruption and renewal; abandoning religious dogmas

Religious renewal calls people back to the spiritual core of earlier Revelations while setting aside doctrines and customs that have obscured it. Bahá’í writings describe each new Revelation as renewing religion for its age, preserving enduring moral truths while changing social laws suited to different conditions. Renewal is not contempt for past religions: it affirms their truth and seeks to recover their original spiritual purpose.

The distinction between enduring and changing aspects of religion explains why social laws can be renewed while moral foundations remain. Progressive revelation and the truth of past religions connect renewal with continuity among divine Revelations. Interpretive questions, sacred scriptures, and the oneness of religion concern how apparent differences may be understood. Links to Buddhism and Hinduism and to other religious traditions extend the discussion across faiths; cycles and spiritual springtime express renewal through recurring imagery. The law of transformation and change is a broader parallel, while calamity and heedlessness describe conditions that can precede or obstruct renewal.
