# parliaments; constitutional government

Constitutional government gives representation a framework of law and accountability, helping secure justice and restrain arbitrary power. Bahá’í teachings also envision a world legislature whose representatives would enact laws for humanity’s common welfare while member states retain autonomy. National constitutional arrangements and the proposed institutions of a future world commonwealth are related but distinct.

Political unity among governments describes the wider commonwealth in which parliaments would cooperate, while the relationship between government and people concerns reciprocal civic responsibilities. Iran’s constitutional history supplies a particular national setting for these principles. The connected themes are related stages and scales of governance, from national representation to international unity.
