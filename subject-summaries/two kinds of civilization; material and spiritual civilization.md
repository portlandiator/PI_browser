# two kinds of civilization; material and spiritual civilization

Material civilization advances through sciences, institutions, industry, and the organization of society, but it cannot by itself secure human happiness or moral purpose. Spiritual civilization gives direction to those capacities through virtues, justice, and recognition of humanity’s oneness. Bahá’í writings call for both dimensions to develop together: material progress is valuable, yet its fruits depend on spiritual principles and their application in social life.

Teaching the Cause connects this distinction with the work of sharing principles that can guide social advancement, while the spiritual path focuses on the individual transformation that gives moral substance to progress. The two are complementary scales of development: one concerns civilization’s collective conditions, the other the person’s inward orientation. Material organization alone does not guarantee justice or unity; spiritual development likewise calls for expression in constructive social life.
