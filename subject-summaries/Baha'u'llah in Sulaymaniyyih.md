# Baha'u'llah in Sulaymaniyyih

Bahá’u’lláh withdrew to the Sulaymaniyyih mountains in Iraqi Kurdistan from 1854 to 1856, living in relative solitude and engaging in prayer and contemplation. During this period he was known to some as a dervish and was not publicly recognized by the Bábís. His return to Baghdad restored unity and direction to the community. The retreat forms a quiet but significant episode between his early ministry and later declaration.

[Bahá’u’lláh](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=3491B0317F6A4084995CD331AD2C278E) situates the retreat within his broader life. [Suffering and imprisonment](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=370AC40D2874E1246B99EB8122E23C9A) connects the period with the hardships of exile, while distinguishing voluntary withdrawal from forced confinement. Together these links clarify how solitude and adversity formed different parts of his experience.
