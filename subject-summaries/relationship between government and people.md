# relationship between government and people

Government and people depend on one another for stability and the welfare of society. Bahá’í writings call for cooperation between rulers and populations, justice in the exercise of authority, and public concern for the safety and comfort of those governed. The relationship is reciprocal: governments have duties toward their subjects, while citizens are encouraged to act loyally and constructively. Constitutional institutions can help give this partnership an orderly form.

Iran’s constitutional revolution and history provide a specific setting for discussions of cooperation between government and people. Nonparticipation in partisan politics is distinct from indifference: the linked guidance supports lawful public order while preserving political neutrality. Obedience to authority, the responsibilities of rulers and kings, and constitutional government describe different aspects of legitimate governance. Justice and mercy and reward and punishment connect authority with moral accountability.
