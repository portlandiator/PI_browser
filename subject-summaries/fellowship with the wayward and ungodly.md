# fellowship with the wayward and ungodly

Kindness toward people who act wrongly does not require approving their conduct or exposing oneself to harm. Bahá’í counsel urges fellowship across religious boundaries and compassion toward those who have lost their way, while also recognizing that association can influence character and that prudence may require limits. Correction should be offered with wisdom and care, not contempt, aggression, or blanket exclusion.

This balance joins [love, unity, and fellowship among the friends](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=B002D95B61D1427697DF9636DF3B6E0A) with [consorting kindly with people of all religions](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=F8339A44D4ED49B8A05EF89719DBCE80). Warm association is meant to overcome estrangement; sound judgment still considers whether a particular relationship encourages harmful conduct or weakens someone’s moral resolve.
