# suffering; rejection of Christ

Jesus was rejected by religious authorities who knew the scriptures, while many ordinary people also struggled to recognize Him. His suffering and death were not defeat: affliction became the cause of His spiritual power and glory, and recognition of His Cause grew after His ministry. The story joins scriptural expectation with the recurring challenge of recognizing a new Revelation.

[Rejection, opposition and persecution](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=E3503B9BB6424884B3157B754204073E) places Christ’s experience within a recurring history of resistance to religious movements. [Literal interpretation](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=87CAE620F6194480B20F2C8B053A5841) connects the topic with the symbolic reading of scriptural accounts. [Suffering and imprisonment](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=370AC40D2874E1246B99EB8122E23C9A) broadens the theme to hardship endured in the path of faith, while [Prophecy and fulfillment](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=A72CD9234D5E4386A7C153F8E08CF797) relates rejection to scriptural expectation. [Rejection of Muhammad](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=78EEE69EFBD4EAB9E0EF7A643386FA7E) shows a parallel pattern in another religious history. Together these links connect Christ’s suffering with persecution, interpretation, and recurring responses to revelation.
