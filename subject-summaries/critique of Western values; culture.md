# critique of Western values; culture

Critiques of Western culture address materialism, exclusiveness, overwork, and the loss of spiritual awareness, while also commenting on social customs and political conduct. These judgments arise in particular historical and cultural settings and should not be turned into blanket claims about all Western people. Their recurring concern is that material achievement alone cannot secure justice, spiritual life, or human happiness.

The East–West topic places these criticisms beside calls for mutual learning and cooperation. Materialism names the spiritual cost of treating outward prosperity as sufficient; the linked discussion of wealth inequality points to economic extremes within that critique. Comparisons with Eastern culture reveal that Bahá’í texts also criticize failings there, rather than idealizing one region. Spiritual susceptibilities and the relation between material and spiritual existence show what may be lost through exclusive material focus, while worldliness describes attachment that turns attention away from spiritual purpose.
