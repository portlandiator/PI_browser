# universal peace; world unity

Universal peace is a condition in which humanity’s security and well-being rest on unity among peoples and nations. Bahá’í writings treat peace as more than the absence of war: it calls for justice, cooperation, and institutions able to address international relations. The work is collective and gradual, involving both changes in human attitudes and the creation of structures that can protect the common good.

The oneness of humanity is the moral basis for world unity; the Lesser and Most Great Peace describe stages in its realization. War and its prevention make the urgency plain, while enumerations of Bahá’í principles and teaching connect peace with the dissemination of its foundations. Political unity and world institutions give peace an organizational dimension, including legislatures, executives, and tribunals. These developments require both spiritual principle and practical efforts; none should be reduced to a single mechanism or timetable.
