# Qur'an and Hadith quotation and interpretation

The Qur’an and Hadith carry religious authority in Islam; interpreting them requires attention to wording, context, transmission, and the distinction between literal and symbolic meaning. Bahá’í interpretation draws on these sources to explain continuity between Revelations and Islamic prophecy. A verse’s direct teaching and its wider spiritual significance are related but distinct uses.

[Interpretation of words and passages in scripture](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=58B72013D935E92718D07A6AB1DCFB78) concerns the methods by which sacred language is understood. [Symbolism](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=BF2B7E937407E648DD80C2528263A450) addresses figurative meanings, while [Soul; spirit after death](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=79CD788B08C5E8B37070B3DD452FB313) connects scriptural interpretation with theological questions. [Stories; anecdotes](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=7A8DB05467DBEF2954FD9B30F84AA0E8) relates quotation to narrative context, and [Humor; jokes](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=8D9F026E4760E4C06FB3125FB37BD27E) acknowledges varied genres within religious discourse. Together these links show that Qur’anic and Hadith interpretation involves textual method, symbolism, doctrine, and context.
