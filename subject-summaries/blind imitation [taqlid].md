# blind imitation [taqlid]

Blind imitation accepts inherited beliefs or customs without examining whether they are true. Bahá’í teaching calls each person to investigate reality independently, while honoring the spiritual insights preserved in earlier religions. The point is not to reject tradition wholesale, but to avoid prejudice and inherited assumptions that prevent people from recognizing truth or treating others justly.

The link with [suffering and imprisonment](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=370AC40D2874E1246B99EB8122E23C9A) reflects how inherited religious or social convictions can lead to persecution. [Prejudice and class distinction](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=9D6982B984B945D3A13E98CA9FB0FE57) shows the social harm of unexamined bias, while [oneness and unity of religion](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=5F1349F0A072481181E9D852C41EC6BD) and [the truth of past religions and scriptures](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=193E825FA96F443BBA9ABC0E163E0F11) preserve continuity across traditions. Independent inquiry is the remedy: [investigation of truth](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=30A42A15DD044876BA9A0BA8D1D60330) requires each person to examine reality directly.
