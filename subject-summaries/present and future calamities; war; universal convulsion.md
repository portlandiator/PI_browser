# present and future calamities; war; universal convulsion

Wars and social upheavals reveal a world order under strain, and warnings of further calamities call for urgent spiritual and social renewal. Prophetic interpretation, reported predictions, and counsels to act practically have different tones and authority; they should not be collapsed into one certain timetable. The immediate response is to relieve suffering and build justice and unity.

War and peace offers the direct connection between catastrophe and efforts to establish lasting peace. The history of integration and disintegration links crises with possible renewal, while rejection and persecution show how opposition can intensify hardship. Corruption and renewal addresses religious and social change; heedlessness and materialism describe spiritual conditions that may worsen disorder. Calls to action, kings’ responsibilities, and divine justice turn attention toward human response and accountability. Grief acknowledges the human cost, while prophecies concern expectations that must be read with care. Cause and effect is a broader philosophical connection, not a specific prediction about particular disasters.
