# growth of the Cause

The growth of the Bahá’í Faith includes individual teaching, community formation, elected institutions, and the increasing capacity to serve society. Bahá’í writings describe this development as both spiritual and organizational: communities learn unity and consultation as they expand. Growth is not measured only in numbers, and it does not remove difficulty or imperfection. It calls for perseverance, sound administration, and sustained service to people beyond the Bahá’í community.

Entry by troops describes a particular pattern of wider participation within the broader growth of the Cause. Teaching, spiritual gatherings, and the travels of ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá are linked as ways the Faith spreads and communities deepen. The Word of God and the greatness of the Day supply spiritual context, while unity, transformation, and grace concern qualities that sustain growth. Opposition, martyrdom, and trials mark hardship; steadfastness and praise encourage persistence. Links to Western culture and East–West communication place growth in geographic contexts. Apostles of Christ, prophecy, and predictions offer historical or scriptural parallels, not identical processes.
