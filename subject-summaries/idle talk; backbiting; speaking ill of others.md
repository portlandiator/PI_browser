# idle talk; backbiting; speaking ill of others

Backbiting and idle talk injure trust, distract from purposeful work, and can turn gatherings into sources of division. Bahá’í guidance calls for speech that is truthful, kind, and useful, and counsels silence when words would only inflame disagreement. The prohibition reaches malicious talk about absent people as well as trivial conversation that consumes attention without helping anyone.
