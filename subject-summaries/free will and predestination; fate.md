# free will and predestination; fate

Free will and predestination concern how human choice relates to divine knowledge, decree, and the order of events. People act within conditions they did not choose while retaining meaningful agency and responsibility. Trust in God and acceptance of what cannot be changed are therefore paired with deliberate action and moral choice.

The practical response to events connects with [contentment and resignation](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=8143807DA046EA3DD9AFADD97E824E93) and [the power of prayer](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=374926D1B4A0440A807608CC09170687), while the problem of suffering and divine justice is developed in [theodicy](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=9DEC6BF8F69A406FADF3FCA1C4BFAE33). Together these subjects frame acceptance as trust amid limits, not as denial of human responsibility or the duty to act.
