# coming-of-age; maturity of mankind

Humanity’s coming of age describes a collective transition from social adolescence toward greater maturity, wisdom, and responsibility. Bahá’í writings use this developmental analogy to explain why inherited political arrangements and habits may no longer meet humanity’s needs. Maturity does not arrive automatically or all at once: it requires people and nations to accept responsibility, learn consultation, and build institutions capable of expressing the oneness of the human race.

Human unity and world peace are central expressions of collective maturity. Expanding knowledge, arts, and technology provides another sign of humanity’s growing capacities, while the gradual development of those capacities cautions against expecting instant transformation. The analogy concerns social evolution, not a literal age assigned to each person or culture.
