# law of transformation and change

Change and transformation are intrinsic to contingent existence: bodies grow, decay, and recombine, while species and societies pass through stages. Material forms change, but some accounts distinguish that change from an enduring essence or identity. Development also has limits: a being may attain perfection within its own degree without becoming another kind of being. Renewal, decline, and progress thus describe different movements within an ordered world.

The limit of development connects to [progress only takes place within its own degree](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=9155EB028D2B4FB3BD41D535FAA264A2): growth can perfect a being without changing its essential rank. The [attaining good pleasure of God](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=034E6E7433614B24B4A7683E29D0C241) relates progress to spiritual purpose and divine assistance. [Law of burial and cremation](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=9D225CE8D12B4E9A839E9EB4935226A2) concerns the transformation and return of bodily elements, while [historical episodes and the lessons of the past](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=EFCE6C7ADF264310AFD8C5A360881252) links change to cycles of events from which people may learn. Finally, [corruption and renewal; abandoning religious dogmas](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=A3B6DA84931BE1A9E2A91755E047538C) applies transformation to religious and social life, where a new revelation can renew inherited forms. These connections range from physical change to spiritual development and historical renewal.
