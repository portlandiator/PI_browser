# suffering and imprisonment

Suffering and imprisonment mark the trials endured by religious figures and their followers, often through exile, confinement, or persecution. Such hardship is not treated as good in itself; its spiritual meaning lies in steadfastness, sacrifice, and the persistence of faith under pressure. Historical accounts of suffering also preserve the human cost of religious conflict.

Accounts of hardship include [‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s life](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=E9694E11B244E2587DE1BB0F8D416710), [Bahá’u’lláh’s Ascension](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=2AA92E315F90E73014DE6159A4A9C633), and [His time in Sulaymaniyyih](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=2E0BDF1117A9E8E28B95887851BB65EC), with comparisons to [Christ](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=0FA444A710AD4FADBE7A093449A77252).
