# the Lesser Peace and the Most Great Peace

Bahá’í writings distinguish the Lesser Peace, a political settlement among nations, from the Most Great Peace, a deeper order shaped by the spiritual and social principles of Bahá’u’lláh. The first can emerge through the actions of governments; the second depends on a wider transformation of humanity and the operation of Bahá’í institutions. The stages are related, but they are not interchangeable, and the Lesser Peace does not by itself complete the work of world unity.

War and peace supplies the immediate historical concern, while universal peace and world unity describe the larger goal toward which both stages point. The Lesser Peace is associated with political efforts among states; the Most Great Peace is linked to the teachings and institutions of the Faith. The distinction prevents a political agreement from being mistaken for the full spiritual and social transformation envisioned in the writings.
