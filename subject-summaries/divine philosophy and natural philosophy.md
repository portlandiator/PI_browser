# divine philosophy and natural philosophy

Natural philosophy investigates physical phenomena; divine philosophy explores spiritual realities and the purpose of human life. Both call for inquiry, reason, and a willingness to seek truth beyond inherited assumptions. Material knowledge and spiritual understanding can develop together, like two wings supporting flight. Their value also appears in conduct: a philosophy that deepens understanding should help people live wisely, serve others, and join intellectual discovery with moral growth.

Philosophical inquiry involves ways of knowing that include the [two kinds of knowledge (ilm and irfan); sight and insight](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=1A11545F6FCC460995486A8C10B9506C): natural philosophy attends to observable attributes, while divine philosophy also seeks spiritual realities. The link to [evolution; human evolution](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=F7EC8954BFA749B4ACA3C4353E8A96CB) reflects passages that describe development through successive levels of existence, a question shared by accounts of nature and human life. The [passage of elementary matter through degrees of existence](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=34D3884EDFE04E158B8879D2E51BA67A) likewise connects the study of natural processes with broader questions about how forms and capacities unfold. These links bring the philosophical distinction into contact with epistemology and developmental accounts of the world.
