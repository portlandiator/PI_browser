# nationalism; love of country

Love of country can inspire care for one’s neighbors and service to the place where one lives, but Bahá’í writings warn against nationalism that places a nation’s interests above humanity. The desired horizon is world citizenship: loyalty to one’s country joined with recognition that all peoples belong to one human family. National identity need not disappear, but it must not become a source of prejudice, rivalry, or war.

Prejudice and class distinction show how national loyalties can combine with other forms of exclusion. Oneness of humanity and universal peace offer a wider basis for belonging, while war reveals the cost of competitive national interests. Oneness of religion connects this unity with shared spiritual foundations across faiths.
