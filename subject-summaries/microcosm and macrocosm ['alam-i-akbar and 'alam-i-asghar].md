# microcosm and macrocosm ['alam-i-akbar and 'alam-i-asghar]

The human being as microcosm reflects, in miniature, patterns associated with the larger cosmos. This analogy links inner and outer worlds, small and vast scales, and the human soul’s place within creation. It is a way of interpreting correspondences, not a claim that a person literally contains the physical universe. The image helps explain why human nature can serve as a lens for considering wider realities, while leaving the two scales distinct.

[The first and the last; the seen and the hidden](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=BD766C442B19416EA9D42C62C5DA721A) places cosmic opposites within a wider account of reality. [Man is the sum; pinnacle; fruit of creation](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=DBA5068DF2C24AE6A61E7337FF0B529D) similarly considers humanity as reflecting many aspects of creation. [Interconnectedness; all things involved in all things; chain of being](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=2FC11A99D6A34DFD93993D59F31450E8) gives the analogy a relational basis across scales, while [the human soul](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=65B32249C87F46A6BEA80B4FE5F9C1EE) locates the human inner life within the comparison. Together these links connect the microcosm image to cosmic patterns, human nature, and the relations joining different levels of existence.
