# creation of the world

Creation is attributed to God’s command, will, and word, through which the heavens, earth, and living beings come into existence. Images range from a single utterance bringing the world forth to layered accounts of elements, spheres, and celestial motion. Creation serves a purpose: knowledge of God and the realization of divine purpose. They include both symbolic and cosmological language, without reducing every account to one mechanism.

The act of creation raises the question of [eternality vs. origination; creation from nothingness](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=BFA8DCA4C0C24066BAF0F869C88CF522): some passages describe a world brought from nothingness, while others stress an eternal process of creation. The [Primal Will](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=9D9002D8700F4A2D893B0869236D586F) appears as the agency through which created forms come into being. Its role connects to [the self-generation of the Primal Will](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=C470397E9BCC44ECB45F21E1521E18C1), which addresses how this creative intermediary itself relates to origination. These subjects approach creation through its beginning, its continuing cause, and the metaphysical account of the Will.
