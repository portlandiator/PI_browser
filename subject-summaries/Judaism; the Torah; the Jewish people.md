# Judaism; the Torah; the Jewish people

The Torah and the Prophets of Israel preserve the history of a people whose religious life shaped later Revelations. Bahá’í teachings affirm Moses and the truth of His religion, connecting biblical prophecy and Israel’s covenantal history with hopes for the Jewish people’s future. Mosaic laws such as fasting and intermarriage also receive attention; Bahá’í interpretations do not replace the diversity of Jewish readings and practice.

[Quotation from or interpretation of the Bible](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=1EBA961BF35048008A08B489196F1A6E) directly addresses scriptural reading, and [Literal interpretation](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=87CAE620F6194480B20F2C8B053A5841) contrasts surface readings with symbolic meaning. [Power; greatness; centrality of the Covenant](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=628D2EB989914C2DBBDDFEB888CA31CA) places covenant within a wider religious and institutional frame. Together these links show how Jewish texts and history are approached through narrative, interpretation, and covenant.
