# two kinds of knowledge [ilm and irfan]; sight and insight

‘Ilm and ‘irfan distinguish forms of knowledge that may be rendered as learning and spiritual recognition or insight. The topic contrasts information about realities with inward perception and lived understanding, while allowing reason and disciplined study a central role. Sight and insight serve as images for the difference between outward observation and spiritual discernment.

The relation between forms of inquiry connects with [divine and natural philosophy](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=43F0402F0B1F4706BC577B7A7A79BFFD) and [harmony of science and religion](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=99656E1B1D7344D8930ACA530FDE210B). Knowledge as practice is related to [walking the spiritual path](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=DBB07A9A48FD421E8F6E02D219149549), while inward discernment also depends on [spiritual emotions and susceptibilities](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=14C5477A6EE041A3B19DAD048E3FBB5B). Together these subjects frame knowledge as intellectual investigation joined with moral and spiritual perception.
