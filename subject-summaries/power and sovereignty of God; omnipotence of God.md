# power and sovereignty of God; omnipotence of God

God creates and sustains all things, and no earthly power can frustrate His purpose. His sovereignty endures while the dominion of rulers passes away; neither human acceptance nor rejection adds to or diminishes it. Divine might acts with wisdom, mercy, and forbearance, strengthening the weak and bringing joy from sorrow. Trust in this power calls for courage and faithful action: hearts can be renewed, and the work of revelation can advance even through opposition and imprisonment.

[God’s absolute freedom and independence](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=82A5773ED5884220A919FDF77F608B7B) explain why His sovereignty depends on no creature, while [His transcendence and unknowability](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=F3B3303C979E42428EFC5629B545E4E4) prevent us from reducing divine power to a human conception of strength. The [names and attributes of God](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=9AB88215A3AC410692D350AE3BEB3A54) connect might with mercy, wisdom, and bounty, revealed through His Manifestations. The [power of the Manifestation of God](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=0E46A9D425ED4F89AB54563B472E6EF6) appears in the transformation of hearts and the spread of revelation despite outward weakness; when [a Manifestation speaks in the voice of Divinity](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=618FD8010F10E760CBC53B05C0F1DC25), divine command and sovereignty find expression in His words. Recognition of this authority leads to [obedience to the Manifestations](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=10C0903A3A7048C193248AAA8629D4D4), offered for love of God’s beauty. [Justice and wrath](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=9170438CF5C0E2D84968B1DE41C36F0F) supply a further dimension: oppression carries consequences, and divine patience expresses forbearance rather than inability to act.
