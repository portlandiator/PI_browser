# corruption and decline of Islam; of the Shi'ih

Discussions of decline in Islam often distinguish the vitality of the religion’s spiritual teachings from later institutions or practices that may depart from them. Bahá’í writings place religious renewal within a broader history of revelation, while particular critiques address specific Shi’i beliefs or authorities. Such claims require careful attribution: criticism of institutions or doctrines should not be generalized to all Muslims or treated as a rejection of Islam itself.

[Historical episodes and the lessons of the past](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=EFCE6C7ADF264310AFD8C5A360881252) provides a frame for understanding institutional change over time. [Critique of Eastern values; culture](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=F9C794BCA51BED7F0C2DC8620BC743F9) connects religious practices with broader cultural judgments, and [Veiling](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=7A3285F9D060E1FFC9B67CEF57102DA7) offers a specific example of debates about custom and religious life. These links call for distinguishing critique of particular traditions or institutions from judgments about Muslims as a whole.
