# acquiescence and resignation; contentment

Acquiescence, resignation, and contentment mean accepting hardship or an outcome without bitterness, while remaining thankful and trusting in God. An unhappy mood can yield to contented submission, and divine decree can be met with patience. This response is not indifference: it allows grief and difficulty to be borne without surrendering hope, agency, or the desire to act well.

Contentment is tested by [suffering and trials](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=CC093CE37A394C1F81BEAF67251D3FC4), supported by [consolation](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=14BC9A91715CE69B9FADD0DC5735323F), and framed by questions of [free will and fate](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=61112CABECB646B7A648800B633240E7). It is strengthened through [detachment](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=379BEFFCBC0C4B9683034D7269624B3C), learned in [stories](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=7A8DB05467DBEF2954FD9B30F84AA0E8), and made visible in the [conduct of true believers](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=0DB1352636234AF3A5E69C0111872F10).
