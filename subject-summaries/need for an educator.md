# need for an educator

Education develops capacities that would otherwise remain latent or incomplete. It includes bodily training, learning for practical and intellectual life, and spiritual formation; no single kind can answer every human need. Teachers help draw out potential, while the quality and purpose of education shape how knowledge is used. In Bahá’í thought, education is a foundation for individual development and social progress, with spiritual education giving human capacities an ethical direction.

[Manifestation of God as educator](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=54135CB53EEE434D8B77F1C91F81A26C) identifies a spiritual role for divine messengers in training humanity. [Essential distinction of Manifestation of God from others](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=4657EFC24412422099AA26E26F483158) marks the unique station associated with that role. [Universal education](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=94AE855C19A34B0D9F7D09FA46079FB3) extends the concern to access for all people, and [education and the development of capacity](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=EEEFCF8268224145B6028CD4848913D1) focuses on how different forms of learning cultivate what is already potential. Together these links distinguish the educator’s spiritual station, the universal scope of learning, and education’s practical effect on human development.
