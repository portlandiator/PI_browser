# four; five pathways of love

Love embraces several relationships: God's love for His own Essence and for creation, humanity's love for God, and the love that joins human beings. Fourfold and fivefold classifications distinguish these pathways in different ways. Spiritual love reaches beyond passing attraction and attachment to outward qualities. It transforms character, creates unity, and becomes visible through service, generosity, and care. Its deepest measure lies in what it enables people to become and to do for one another.

The social effects of love are reflected in the [characteristics and conduct of true believers](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=0DB1352636234AF3A5E69C0111872F10), where devotion takes shape through how people treat one another. The [love of God](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=410D6617D11A4266BA52B4B00A48D649) is a central source and object of these pathways, while [love as fundamental; spiritual foundations of religion](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=C7824F7FB65A4B75A74AC15AFD08EC5C) connects love to religion’s ethical and unifying purpose. One classification begins with [God's love for His own Essence](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=592F9B4AA5B5491F827732B4D4675681) and traces love outward toward creation and human relationships. Together these subjects show love as divine source, inward orientation, and lived bond; the four- and fivefold schemes remain distinct formulations in the source material.
