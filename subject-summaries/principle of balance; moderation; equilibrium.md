# principle of balance; moderation; equilibrium

Balance and moderation guide conduct by avoiding excess and preserving conditions for well-being. Equilibrium applies to individual choices as well as relationships among material, social, and spiritual concerns. Moderation does not mean passivity or a fixed midpoint in every situation; it calls for proportion suited to the matter at hand. The principle helps connect practical restraint with health, simplicity, and a civilization that joins outward achievement to inner development.

[Moderation; frugality; simplicity](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=02B4E611F3EC4C52AD11023CE5588FA7) gives the principle practical form through restraint and simplicity. [Health and healing; material and spiritual healing](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=FDA358C85EABE1A21EBBED1872E92D55) shows balance across prayer and medical care, with both spiritual support and competent treatment valued. [Materialism; material vs. spiritual civilization](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=C32DCE1484C6E34B581C0D0C738B7E27) extends the question to society, where external progress alone is insufficient without moral development. Together these links present moderation as a way to coordinate different needs rather than reject one side of life.
