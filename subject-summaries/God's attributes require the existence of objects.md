# God's attributes require the existence of objects

A Creator implies creation; a Provider implies beings who receive provision. God's names express an active generosity whose outpouring never ceases. His perfections are eternally expressed, rather than lying dormant until a particular moment. The image of a sun inseparable from its rays conveys this enduring relationship. It concerns the expression of divine attributes in creation, without making God's unknowable Essence dependent on the creatures that receive His bounty.

[Names and attributes](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=9AB88215A3AC410692D350AE3BEB3A54) provide the starting point for this subject, while [essential and actional attributes](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=B8ED66ABF42C4C94B1950954F56B46F0) distinguish God's own being from His relationship with creation. [God's knowledge](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=D49918E5C4014E13A7AD43BCB1E89561) tests the limits of the argument: we cannot simply assume that divine knowing requires objects in the same way human knowing does. [Knowledge of attributes as knowledge of God](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=7A7BDCD83AE14B19B4D78332D456B1D3) explains why we nevertheless approach Him through these relationships and their effects. [Ceaseless divine emanation](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=68137F318C6144AAAF17C8290F33061E) and [infinity and eternity](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=6BB666C0D9224ED7AE3400FC770A3496) extend the theme toward the unending character of creation and grace. [Eternal archetypes](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=5C9B2EC0371C49F7A3566365EC1285B8) raise a further question about things known before their appearance, but their selection includes criticism of placing a plurality of forms within God's Essence. These connections preserve the argument's force without treating it as a complete account of the divine reality.
