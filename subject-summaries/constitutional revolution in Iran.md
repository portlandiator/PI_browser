# constitutional revolution in Iran

Iran’s Constitutional Revolution brought a struggle over political authority and constitutional government into the early twentieth century. Bahá’í guidance on the upheaval emphasizes the community’s nonpartisan stance while encouraging loyalty, obedience to government, and concern for the welfare of the people. This Iranian crisis raises enduring questions about how believers should act amid political change.

The topic of nonparticipation in politics clarifies the Bahá’í guidance connected to this revolution: believers are counseled not to join partisan factions, while remaining concerned with public welfare and obeying legitimate authority. The relationship between government and people adds a complementary civic perspective, addressing mutual responsibilities rather than endorsing a faction in the constitutional struggle. These links help distinguish political counsel from a claim that the upheaval itself had a single religious interpretation.
