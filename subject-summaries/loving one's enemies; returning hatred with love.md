# loving one's enemies; returning hatred with love

Love of enemies replaces retaliation with mercy, patience, and active goodwill. It asks a person to answer injury with kindness, insult with respect, and hatred with sincere concern, while refusing to let resentment govern the heart. This ethic extends beyond tolerating an adversary: it treats the enemy as a potential friend and brother. The standard is demanding, but it follows the example of Christ and the principle that divine mercy reaches both just and unjust.

This ethic belongs to the [conduct of true believers](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=0DB1352636234AF3A5E69C0111872F10) and widens into [kindness toward all people](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=F8339A44D4ED49B8A05EF89719DBCE80). Particular guidance may come through [personal instructions](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=D639D12CD679E3ED02A0F7A8D7835DB2), but its practice depends on [forgiveness and forbearance](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=F28151459776E3BE109C95C138177810). Such responses are part of the [virtuous spiritual path](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=DBB07A9A48FD421E8F6E02D219149549), where [love and unity](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=79CCBA0D8726E9D920DC566B92556700) overcome estrangement.
