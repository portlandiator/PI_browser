# rejection by the people of the Bayan

Many followers of the Báb’s Bayan turned away when Bahá’u’lláh declared His mission, despite the Bayan’s anticipation of the promised Manifestation. Bahá’u’lláh’s lament connects their rejection with inherited expectations and the influence of rival leaders. The episode marks a painful turning point in Bábí history and underscores the demand to recognize the new Revelation when it appears.

[Bahá’u’lláh](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=3491B0317F6A4084995CD331AD2C278E)’s life supplies the historical center of this rejection: He is the One whose claim the passages say was refused. The [proclamation to the people of the Bayan](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=E24FE7CCD7B1E27FD3CD60548C20CE2D) addresses that community directly, urging recognition of the promised Manifestation. [Covenant-breaking](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=42BA11FC5FE24CBEA769298634A7FA42) connects the episode with disputes over authority and fidelity after Bahá’u’lláh’s appearance; the link concerns a related pattern of opposition, not an identity between all Bábís and later Covenant-breakers. The [Word of God](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=4E323D7B85E34704B18BD5AB68F84E22) is central because the passages appeal to the Bayan’s own revealed guidance as evidence that its followers should recognize the One it foretold.
