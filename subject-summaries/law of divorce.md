# law of divorce

Bahá’í divorce is generally delayed for a year when affection has broken down, allowing spouses to live apart while time and counsel may restore harmony. Divorce becomes permissible if aversion persists after the waiting year; reconciliation ends the separation period. Some conditions can alter the procedure, and the writings discuss responsibilities during the year and the settlement of the marriage. The waiting period is intended to protect the possibility of reunion, not to trap spouses in a relationship where harmony cannot return.
