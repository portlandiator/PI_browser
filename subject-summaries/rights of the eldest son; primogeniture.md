# rights of the eldest son; primogeniture

Primogeniture concerns the inheritance rights assigned to the eldest son, especially the family residence under the Bahá’í laws of inheritance. The rule is part of a larger system that distributes property among family members and other designated recipients. It should be understood within that legal framework rather than as a general statement that sons have greater human worth. The specific subject here is a defined inheritance provision.

[Law of inheritance](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=45F19A41FDE542EAA950F0554A7B175A) provides the wider legal context: the cited example allocates shares among several classes of heirs and includes special provisions for children. Primogeniture addresses one particular element of that system, the family residence and its associated buildings. Reading the two topics together distinguishes a specific inheritance right from the broader distribution of an estate.
