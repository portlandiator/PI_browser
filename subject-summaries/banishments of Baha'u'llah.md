# banishments of Baha'u'llah

Bahá’u’lláh was repeatedly exiled by Persian and Ottoman authorities, moving from Tehran to Baghdad, Constantinople, Adrianople, and finally ‘Akká. Each banishment brought hardship and separation from his followers, while also expanding the reach of his writings and teachings. Exile shaped the geography of the Bahá’í Faith and the conditions under which Bahá’u’lláh’s mission unfolded. His successive journeys remain central to the history of the religion.

[Bahá’u’lláh](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=3491B0317F6A4084995CD331AD2C278E) supplies the overall life context. [Banishment to; life in ‘Akká](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=4FAFE46E3FC7417BAC3D18A3C7D260C2) and [Banishment to; life in Adrianople](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=6580A1098A7242F9B29D4916F5B4F414) focus on particular stages of exile, while [Suffering and imprisonment](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=370AC40D2874E1246B99EB8122E23C9A) describes the hardships accompanying them. Together these links connect the broad sequence of banishments with specific places and the experience of persecution.
