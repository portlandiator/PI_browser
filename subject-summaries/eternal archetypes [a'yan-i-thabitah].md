# eternal archetypes [a'yan-i-thabitah]

In philosophical accounts of creation, a‘yan-i-thabitah are fixed archetypes or possibilities associated with divine knowledge. Their status is debated: some formulations describe known forms in divine knowledge, while others reject placing distinct objects within the divine Essence. The return of a rose each year illustrates how qualities can recur without the return of the same individual identity. The technical terminology includes provisional translations.

The archetypes are placed within the distinction between [God’s oneness in ahadiyyih and wahidiyyih](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=6B2E2534F05E41069B3E1A18F5B552D4), where undifferentiated oneness is distinguished from articulation through names and realities. The question of whether attributes imply objects is taken up by [God’s attributes requiring the existence of objects](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=50E64D5F25E34797A55642F2919C4317), a debate that bears directly on theories of divine knowledge and eternal forms. [God’s love for His own Essence](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=592F9B4AA5B5491F827732B4D4675681) connects through passages that describe self-knowledge and love within divine unity. Together these links show why the subject turns on careful distinctions between the divine Essence, its knowledge, and the contingent realities described in philosophical vocabulary.
