# reward and punishment

Reward and punishment help sustain social order: just consequences can restrain oppression and encourage responsible conduct. Bahá’í writings call rulers to uphold justice among their subjects, while distinguishing justice from vengeance and mercy from impunity. The principle applies to institutions and moral life, where fairness and accountability protect the conditions needed for peace and human well-being.

Justice is central to this subject, and the relationship between justice and mercy clarifies why accountability must be balanced with compassion. Religion as a basis of morality connects ethical conduct with social order. Government and people brings the principle into civic life: rulers are accountable for how they treat those they govern.
