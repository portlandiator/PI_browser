# non-participation in politics

Bahá’ís are called to avoid partisan political activity while remaining loyal citizens and working for the betterment of society. Non-participation in party politics is not withdrawal from public concern; it reflects a commitment to unity and an independent spiritual identity amid political divisions. The principle coexists with respect for government authority, obedience to law, and constructive service to the common good.

[Constitutional revolution in Iran](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=A25CD3C8159CEB118B7B8E04471AF21A) supplies a historical context in which political change and religious community intersect. [Relationship between government and people](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=8DB0F6A838C1E54167E1BBF27836246B) considers the need for cooperation between state and society, while [Obedience to and respect for government authority](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=F9C86676F30D4FBC8FA75C7F25536147) clarifies the duty of loyalty. Together these connections distinguish non-partisanship from indifference to public welfare or disrespect for lawful government.
