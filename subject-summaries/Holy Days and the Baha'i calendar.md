# Holy Days and the Baha'i calendar

The Bahá’í calendar has nineteen months of nineteen days, with intercalary days before the month of fasting. It begins at Naw-Rúz near the March equinox and names its months after attributes of God. The calendar also marks the Fast and sacred anniversaries, including the two Most Great Festivals. These observances order devotional and community life through a distinct cycle of sacred time.

[Law of fasting](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=0C5DDE6CAB1F402695B2717CF1EC0833) links the calendar to a period of spiritual discipline. [Music and singing](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=4027C84B01D8438E9C7D92535DF6BAB7) and [Spiritual meetings](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=53AFF53F02514681A22F4E2FAB467483) connect observance with worship and fellowship. [Shrines and Holy places](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=69863262A551E3763501D5846A98329B) relates the calendar to sacred geography, while [House of Justice](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=A801789EBB804ABB86C36BB8CAB50FED) concerns the institution responsible for matters of administration, including the calendar’s application. Together these links connect time, worship, place, and governance.
