# Declaration of Baha'u'llah

Bahá’u’lláh’s declaration in the Garden of Ridván in 1863 announced his station to a circle of followers as “Him Whom God shall make manifest.” The event took place as he was preparing to leave Baghdad for Constantinople, turning another forced exile into the beginning of a new religious era. Ridván is commemorated as the greatest Bahá’í festival, marking the public emergence of his mission and the fulfillment of the Báb’s promise.

[Bahá’u’lláh](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=3491B0317F6A4084995CD331AD2C278E) connects the declaration to the full arc of his life and teachings. [Banishment to; life in Constantinople](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=7F59847655ADEAE2D09CC9CC9F5F4DE1) provides its immediate historical setting, as the declaration preceded his departure from Baghdad. Together these links show the event as both a spiritual proclamation and a moment within the sequence of exile.
