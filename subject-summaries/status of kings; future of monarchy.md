# status of kings; future of monarchy

Political authority is a trust: rulers should govern justly, protect the oppressed, and use power for the welfare of their peoples. Bahá’í texts praise kings who serve these ends and describe monarchy as compatible with representative institutions in some settings. The counsel sets ethical limits on rule without endorsing tyranny or prescribing one political form for every society.

Government authority is linked with obedience by citizens, but the passages also insist on rulers’ duties toward the people. The relationship between government and people gives that reciprocity explicit form. Liberty concerns freedom from oppression, and petitions to authorities show how grievances may be brought forward. The future of Haifa and the Holy Land is among the prophetic references to rulers’ future actions; calamity connects political failure with broader crisis.
