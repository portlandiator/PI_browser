# law of burial and cremation

Bahá’í law directs that the dead be buried rather than cremated, treating bodily decomposition as part of the natural order of creation. The writings specify burial practices and include guidance on coffins, burial rings, and funeral expenses. The body returns gradually to its elements, while the soul’s spiritual journey is distinct from the body’s fate. The law rejects both preservation of the corpse as a route to salvation and rapid destruction through cremation, emphasizing respect for the created order.

Burial is related to the [law of transformation and change](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=77FE3431893F4FD6A31E4BC04DA1BEFC), which addresses the natural processes of composition and dissolution.
