# Judas

Judas is remembered in Christian scripture as the disciple who betrayed Jesus, a story that raises questions about choice, loyalty, and responsibility. Bahá’í references to Judas draw on this account while emphasizing that people are not compelled to reject a prophet; actions have moral consequences. Interpretations of Judas should remain grounded in the scriptural narrative rather than expanding into unsupported claims about his motives or character.

[Covenant-breaking and Covenant-breakers](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=42BA11FC5FE24CBEA769298634A7FA42) concerns the rupture of religious unity and fidelity, a distinct but comparable theme of betrayal. [Avoidance of Covenant-Breakers](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=1A971A1344BAEBC011C3866BFE27B179) addresses the consequences of such a breach within Bahá’í community life. [Saint Peter and Saint Paul](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=4A93A1AFA0B44305BB29CFD7BF83CFC4) offers a contrast in the varied responses of Christ’s followers, including denial, repentance, and later service. Together these links connect betrayal with questions of loyalty and response.
