# attaining good pleasure of God

Attaining God’s good pleasure means seeking a life acceptable in divine sight, through faith, conduct, and obedience rather than worldly status or possessions. The prayers ask that actions yield what pleases God and that believers recognize which choices draw them nearer. Human effort matters, but it cannot claim the result as earned: grace and mercy remain essential. The aim is both practical—how one lives—and inward, a sincere desire to align the heart with divine will.

The concern echoes [many are called but few are chosen](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=1AD5A5F58C9D4D80A982ED7AA5435D7E), since divine favor is offered broadly but not received by all. The aim of acceptance can involve [change and transformation](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=77FE3431893F4FD6A31E4BC04DA1BEFC), the [spirit of faith](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=1AA38915869D4AD5856F5B367531CA0E), and obedience to the [laws of God](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=752881F17C2B4EE0BDA239B9D6517A45). It is animated by [love of God](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=410D6617D11A4266BA52B4B00A48D649) and expressed through [good deeds](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=318AF6512E454733BC17A1B298A41946), while ultimately depending on [grace and forgiveness](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=5CF47B6B008F461986A90EF7E6BF176E), not merit alone.
