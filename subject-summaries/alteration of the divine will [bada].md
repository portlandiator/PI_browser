# alteration of the divine will [bada]

Badá’ names divine spontaneity or freedom to act in ways that may alter an expected outcome or the fulfillment of a promise. Examples of delayed events and changed decrees caution against assuming that human predictions can bind divine action. Divine freedom differs from creaturely change, which arises from limitation. Once an event is manifested in time, it cannot be undone; later events may instead follow it.

The relation between divine freedom and human expectation connects to [tests and trials; sacrifice and suffering](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=CC093CE37A394C1F81BEAF67251D3FC4): an apparent delay or change can test whether people remain patient and receptive. Unexpected outcomes need not imply a failure of divine power or justice. This link highlights the spiritual response to uncertainty alongside the theological question of divine spontaneity.
