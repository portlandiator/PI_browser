# religion as reality; definitions of religion

Religion is a search for reality, not a substitute for reason. When belief conflicts with evidence and investigation, it risks becoming superstition; science and religion should therefore be tested against truth and understood as complementary ways of knowing. Religion also gives moral direction and spiritual purpose, joining individuals and communities in love, unity, and civilization. Its teachings are meant to renew human life, not harden into inherited dogma detached from reality.

This account of religion joins the [harmony of science and religion](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=99656E1B1D7344D8930ACA530FDE210B) with the [oneness of religion](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=5F1349F0A072481181E9D852C41EC6BD). Lists of [Bahá’í principles](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=10F20171888446BAB9501BF1262D49C7) help articulate its teachings, while [renewal beyond corrupted dogma](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=A3B6DA84931BE1A9E2A91755E047538C) relates to the [qualities of a Bahá’í](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=AD7F1E7F696842D3B9D7E108934676B7). Religion’s social role includes a [moral foundation for civilization](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=4F32835BDB1F4C02956E92DB1AE38C5F) and the cultivation of [love and unity](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=FFC8B795919D48FD82F6DD702B6A4A3E).
