# unity of governments; political unity

Political unity among nations is envisioned as a world commonwealth that safeguards the freedom and autonomy of its members while coordinating their shared responsibilities. Bahá’í writings outline institutions such as a world legislature, executive, and tribunal to regulate international affairs, settle disputes, and protect collective security. This vision is not a centralized conquest: it depends on nations’ willing cooperation and aims to unite humanity while preserving cultural and individual diversity.

A world executive and parliament describe key functions of the proposed commonwealth, while a supreme tribunal would adjudicate disputes among its members. Universal peace and world unity express the purpose behind these institutions: coordinated government should protect security and well-being, not erase local autonomy. Together, the links distinguish political architecture from the moral aim it serves.
