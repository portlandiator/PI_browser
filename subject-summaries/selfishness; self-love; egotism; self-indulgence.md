# selfishness; self-love; egotism; self-indulgence

Selfishness makes personal advantage the measure of life and obscures the needs of others. It can corrupt even outwardly virtuous service when recognition, influence, or reward becomes the hidden aim. Freedom from ego grows through turning toward God, examining motives, sharing generously, and working for the common good. Trials can help uncover these subtle attachments, clearing the heart for a love that reaches beyond its own interests.

[Purity of heart and sincerity of intention](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=98B67B70516949FD856C36A52AE23098) answer the central challenge of selfishness: an action's outward goodness does not guarantee freedom from private motives. Examining why one serves, gives, or seeks responsibility helps distinguish devotion from the desire for status. These connected subjects make inward sincerity and practical concern for others inseparable parts of spiritual growth.
