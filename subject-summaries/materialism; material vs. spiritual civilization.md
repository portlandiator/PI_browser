# materialism; material vs. spiritual civilization

Materialism reduces human purpose to physical needs, possessions, and outward achievement. Bahá’í writings criticize a civilization that has impressive institutions and technical powers but lacks cultivated character, justice, and spiritual awareness. They do not reject material well-being; rather, they insist that material means must serve human dignity and be joined to inner virtues if they are to bring lasting peace and happiness.

Critiques of Western culture illustrate how wealth, work, and external sophistication can crowd out spiritual life; worldliness names the inward attachment that sustains this imbalance. Heedlessness connects material preoccupation with unawareness of spiritual reality. Calamity and war are linked as possible consequences of social conditions lacking moral foundations, while the forces of light and darkness set decline against renewal. The principle of moderation offers a corrective: equitable, balanced provision can support well-being without making material accumulation the measure of life.
