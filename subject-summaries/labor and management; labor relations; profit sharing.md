# labor and management; labor relations; profit sharing

Bahá’í writings address labor and management as partners in creating society’s material well-being. Their proposals include fair treatment of workers and profit-sharing, so employees can benefit from the enterprise to which they contribute. The goal is neither class conflict nor unchecked accumulation, but a more just economic relationship that recognizes both the needs of labor and the responsibilities of ownership.

The House of Justice is linked because some passages discuss community institutions and economic arrangements; wealth moderation gives the broader aim of reducing extremes between rich and poor. The maintenance of social degrees recognizes differences in capacity and function without making them a justification for exploitation. These connections frame labor relations as part of a just social order.
