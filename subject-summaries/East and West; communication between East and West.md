# East and West; communication between East and West

East and West are relative directions, not separate human worlds. Bahá’í teachings call for communication, mutual assistance, and the sharing of strengths across regions: learning and spirituality alongside science, technology, and industry. Travel, correspondence, and personal encounters can help dissolve inherited distance and prejudice, allowing diverse peoples to recognize their common humanity and contribute to a wider unity.

Community-building gives this ideal a practical setting: relationships among local communities and institutions can turn goodwill into cooperation. The Word of God is described as the unifying spiritual center, and growth of the Cause carries the message across regions. Spiritual communication and human oneness express the inward and social dimensions of that union. Prophecies, Iran’s history, and critiques of Eastern and Western cultures supply historical contexts; the contrasts are not claims that either region is uniform or without defects. The linked subject of progressive revelation further grounds unity in the shared source of the world’s religions.
