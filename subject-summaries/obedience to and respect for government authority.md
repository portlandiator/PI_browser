# obedience to and respect for government authority

Bahá’í guidance calls believers to obey the government of the country where they live, act with honesty and loyalty, and refrain from partisan political struggle. This obedience has a stated limit: it does not require denying the Faith or violating a spiritual principle. The guidance presents lawful conduct as a way to contribute to public order while keeping the community focused on service and the development of its institutions.

The relationship between government and people places civic obedience within reciprocal responsibilities: authority should serve the welfare of the population, and citizens should act loyally. The status of kings connects with traditional forms of rule discussed in the writings, while nonparticipation in politics clarifies why obedience is not partisan allegiance or political activism.
