# the economic problem and its solution; voluntary giving

Bahá’í teachings address poverty and inequality through justice, care for the poor, and voluntary sharing of wealth. They distinguish generosity freely chosen from redistribution imposed by force, while also recognizing a role for laws that prevent extreme concentrations of wealth and deprivation. Economic well-being depends on both personal moral action and social arrangements that allow every person to live with dignity.

Social and divine justice provide the ethical basis for fair economic conditions, while the moderation of wealth addresses the gap between riches and destitution. Education is connected as a long-term means of developing human capacity and strengthening communities. Voluntary generosity and structural safeguards work at different levels of the same problem; the writings do not treat charity alone as a substitute for just arrangements.
