# four causes

The four causes explain an object through what produces it, what it is made from, the form it takes, and the purpose it serves. The familiar example of a chair identifies a carpenter, wood, the chair’s structure, and its use as a seat. This framework treats existence as dependent on several conditions rather than one undifferentiated cause. It offers a compact way to analyze how an effect comes to be and what sustains its identity.

The four causes refine the broader [law of cause and effect](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=DF7D0EA999F44DBCA4F5C695B3851674). Causality states that effects depend on causes; this scheme distinguishes the different roles those causes play in the existence of a particular thing. The connection moves from a general principle to a structured analysis of origin, material, form, and purpose.
