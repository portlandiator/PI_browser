# greatness and influence of the Cause; of this Day

Bahá’í writings call the Bahá’í era the Day of God: a time of revelation, mercy, and a new spiritual life for humanity. They describe Bahá’u’lláh’s Revelation as having a singular station and far-reaching influence, while urging people to recognize its guidance and respond through service. The Day is not only a subject for celebration; its significance carries responsibilities for personal transformation and the betterment of the world.

The teachings as spirit of the age and the Word of God explain the spiritual renewal associated with this Day. Prophecy and fulfillment connect its claims with earlier expectations, while the Manifestation-as-sun image expresses the source of illumination. The subject of entry by troops addresses collective response and growth, and the linked call to teach, good deeds, and service emphasize active participation. Universal peace and oneness describe social aims; spiritual transformation and equality address human development. Other links treat Iran, opposition, heedlessness, detachment, grace, and the believer’s station. These are distinct dimensions of the Day’s significance, not identical predictions.
