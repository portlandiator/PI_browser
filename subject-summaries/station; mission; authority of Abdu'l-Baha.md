# station; mission; authority of Abdu'l-Baha

‘Abdu’l‑Bahá described His station as servitude to Bahá’u’lláh, while also affirming the authority Bahá’u’lláh conferred upon Him as the authorized interpreter and center of the Covenant. His mission included guiding the community, safeguarding unity, and explaining the sacred writings. Bahá’í texts distinguish His unique authority from the station of a Manifestation of God; humility and delegated authority are held together rather than confused.

The Covenant is the heart of this subject: Bahá’u’lláh appointed ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá, and the linked topics on the Covenant’s centrality, firmness, and role in preserving unity explain why that appointment matters for continuity. The House of Justice is a later institution within the administrative order, distinct from ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá’s personal station. Authentication of sacred writings and infallibility connect to questions of authority and interpretation, while tests of God and the mission of the Manifestation mark important distinctions in rank. The links to humility, servitude, and submission reflect ‘Abdu’l‑Bahá’s chosen language for His service; links to suffering, opposition, and trials recall the circumstances in which He carried out that mission. Covenant-breaking and rejection show the challenges His authority faced.
