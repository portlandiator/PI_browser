# divine unity [tawhid] and degrees of unity

Tawhid affirms God’s incomparable oneness while describing how creation depends upon its Creator and bears signs of divine unity. This distinction rules out confusing creation with God, even as spiritual perception recognizes signs of unity throughout the world. Divine unity also has a revelatory dimension: successive Manifestations share a spiritual oneness while remaining distinct in their historical appearance.

This theme of unity as both transcendence and reflected signs links to [oneness or innumerability of the Manifestations](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=D94FA897F92748119D02B1738E91BD1C), where a single spiritual reality is described through many revelations, and to [God’s oneness in ahadiyyih and wahidiyyih](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=6B2E2534F05E41069B3E1A18F5B552D4), which distinguishes undifferentiated from articulated unity. [The station of the Manifestation of God](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=703AB631CB76E66408E7D6F7160F01F5) also bears on how divine unity is known and expressed in history. Across these connections, the texts preserve a distinction between God and creation even when they describe the world as bearing witness to divine unity; the unity of revelation concerns the Manifestations’ spiritual reality, while the divine Essence remains beyond human comprehension.
