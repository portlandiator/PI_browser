# war and peace

Bahá’í teachings condemn war’s killing and devastation and call humanity toward peace founded on justice and unity. Peace requires more than the end of fighting: it depends on cooperation among peoples, fair social relations, and institutions capable of addressing shared needs. Writings distinguish stages in the establishment of peace and urge believers to work actively for reconciliation while refusing hatred and violence.

The Lesser Peace and Most Great Peace clarify distinct stages in humanity’s movement toward world peace. Universal peace and human oneness describe the broader aim, while justice identifies a necessary foundation and nationalism points to divisions that must be overcome. Call to action connects this ideal with practical effort. Calamities and the forces of integration and disintegration set peace against conflict, and predictions and prophecies concern expectations for humanity’s future. These links make peace a moral and institutional undertaking, not merely a pause between wars.
