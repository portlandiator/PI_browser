# God's knowledge

Nothing escapes God's knowledge, and His knowing is not bound by our divisions of past, present, and future. He knows things before and after they exist without needing to learn or discover them. Yet knowledge of an event is not the cause of its occurrence: foreseeing an eclipse does not produce it. Divine knowledge expresses a perfection beyond human understanding, not a process of observation or reasoning like our own.

[Essential and actional attributes](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=B8ED66ABF42C4C94B1950954F56B46F0) distinguish God's unknowable knowledge in Himself from knowledge considered in relation to the created world. The neighboring subject about [attributes requiring objects](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=50E64D5F25E34797A55642F2919C4317) asks how names such as All-Knowing and All-Seeing relate to things known or seen; it also cautions against assuming that God's knowledge has the same requirements as ours. [Knowledge of attributes as knowledge of God](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=7A7BDCD83AE14B19B4D78332D456B1D3) brings the discussion back to human recognition: divine perfections become accessible through their revelation, especially in the Manifestations, without making the divine Essence comprehensible. Together, these connections distinguish God's knowledge of creation from our knowledge of God, and invite careful use of human analogies without treating them as explanations of His inmost reality.
