# critique of Eastern values; culture

Critiques of Eastern societies address inherited customs, political repression, social divisions, limited education, and restrictions on women. Bahá’í writings also acknowledge capacities and virtues across the region, and call for renewal through justice, education, and spiritual transformation. The criticism is therefore directed at specific conditions and practices, not at the worth of Eastern peoples or cultures as a whole.

East–West communication provides a framework for mutual assistance rather than one-sided judgment, and Iran’s history gives a specific national context to some critiques. The comparisons with Western culture make clear that materialism and social injustice are not confined to one region. References to religious decline connect cultural problems with institutional history, while veiling and the station of women focus on gender restrictions and the call for women’s dignity. These links retain the difference between critiquing a custom and denigrating a people.
