# banishment to; life in Baghdad

Bahá’u’lláh lived in Baghdad from 1853 to 1863, a decade that included his withdrawal to the mountains of Sulaymaniyyih and his eventual declaration in the Garden of Ridván. The period saw the Bábí community recover and grow, while Bahá’u’lláh’s influence became increasingly recognized. Baghdad also became the setting for important writings and correspondence. This stage of exile bridges his early ministry and public declaration.

[Bahá’u’lláh](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=3491B0317F6A4084995CD331AD2C278E) provides the broader account of his life, while [Life in Adrianople](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=6580A1098A7242F9B29D4916F5B4F414) follows the Baghdad period chronologically. [Stories; anecdotes](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=7A8DB05467DBEF2954FD9B30F84AA0E8) reflects narratives about the daily experiences of exile. [Service to others](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=75315FCCBA5D4B54A22808A1159B1775) and [Music and singing](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=4027C84B01D8438E9C7D92535DF6BAB7) connect with the spiritual and communal life associated with this period. Together these links situate Baghdad within the wider sequence of exile and the life of the community.
