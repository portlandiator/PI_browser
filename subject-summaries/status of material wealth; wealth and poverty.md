# status of material wealth; wealth and poverty

Material wealth can support well-being and service, but it does not define a person’s worth or guarantee happiness. Poverty and wealth both carry practical and moral questions: how resources are gained, what needs they meet, and how attachment to possessions shapes life. Material means serve human society best when acquired and used with justice, generosity, and perspective.

[Detachment; severance; renunciation; patience](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=379BEFFCBC0C4B9683034D7269624B3C) weighs attachment to possessions against spiritual purpose. [Conduct in finance and business](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=8EDE6B0C407DE4EE770AB1CEFF0BCA15) concerns how wealth is acquired and managed, and [Work as worship](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=CB73A56CDCC6E0484B0C8324B42C7E3D) connects earning with meaningful labor. [Walking the spiritual path](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=DBB07A9A48FD421E8F6E02D219149549) relates material choices to character, while [Tests and trials](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=CC093CE37A394C1F81BEAF67251D3FC4) considers hardship and sacrifice. [Transience; worthlessness of the physical world](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=CBBEF627F45A45E7850653204203438C) places possessions within a wider view of what endures. Together these links distinguish the use of wealth from attachment to it.
