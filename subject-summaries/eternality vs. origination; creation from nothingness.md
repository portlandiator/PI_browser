# eternality vs. origination; creation from nothingness

The discussion of creation holds together two claims: created things originate through God’s command, and creation has no beginning or end in time. Some texts speak of being brought from nothingness, while others explain that the Creator’s eternal names and powers imply an ever-present creation. “Nothingness” can also serve as symbolic language for dependence, not an independent material state. The topic explores how origination, duration, and divine eternality can be discussed without treating God as a temporal object.

This theme complements [creation of the world](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=E7BFAD48788E4AFE9B6EA752F7671AB1) by focusing on whether bringing-into-being describes a first temporal moment or an ongoing relation between Creator and creation. The [divine emanation is ceaseless; eternal](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=68137F318C6144AAAF17C8290F33061E) link emphasizes a continuing outpouring of divine grace across time. The [self-generation of the Primal Will](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=C470397E9BCC44ECB45F21E1521E18C1) addresses an intermediary principle in accounts of origination. These connections preserve a useful distinction: the texts use “creation from nothingness” alongside claims of creation’s beginningless duration, and do not make those phrases simple synonyms.
