# the golden rule; regard for one's neighbor; doing unto others

The Golden Rule measures conduct by the treatment one would accept oneself: respect others’ rights, do not impose what would cause pain, and choose for a neighbor what one would choose for one’s own good. Bahá’í passages extend reciprocity into self-forgetting service, asking believers at times to prefer others to themselves. The principle applies across ranks and communities, linking personal fairness with brotherhood and the welfare of humanity.

This ethic is one mark of the [conduct of true believers](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=0DB1352636234AF3A5E69C0111872F10) and leads directly into [service to others and the Cause](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=75315FCCBA5D4B54A22808A1159B1775), since regard for a neighbor becomes active concern for that person’s well-being.
