# educational curricula

Educational curricula shape what learners know, how they reason, and the habits they develop. A sound program joins access to knowledge with age-appropriate learning, moral formation, and preparation to contribute to society. Curriculum is not just a list of subjects: it reflects choices about human development and the purpose of education. Those choices should serve learners’ capacities while respecting the broad aims of universal education.

[Universal education](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=94AE855C19A34B0D9F7D09FA46079FB3) establishes the aim of learning for all, while [Education of children; moral education](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=4527819F4272482F94777C3D36326FD4) focuses on how that aim begins in childhood. Both entries for [Serving humanity; working for progress; betterment of the world](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=5A637BBBB913426BA5FEBBC58E0A6BF2) and [Serving humanity; working for progress, betterment of the world](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=5A637BBBB913426BA5FEBBC58E0A6BF2-09862a80) connect education with service and community development. [Walking the spiritual path; spiritual wayfaring; the virtuous life](https://portlandiator.github.io/PI_browser/subjects.html?subject=DBB07A9A48FD421E8F6E02D219149549) adds the cultivation of personal qualities. Together these connections frame curriculum as intellectual, ethical, and civic preparation.
