# executive government; power of execution

A world executive would carry out the decisions and laws of a future international commonwealth. Bahá’í writings describe it as one part of a larger system that includes a representative world legislature and a tribunal for resolving disputes. Its purpose is to uphold collective decisions and protect the unity of nations, while the proposed structure preserves domestic authority for internal affairs. The executive is a political institution, distinct from Bahá’í religious institutions.

Political unity among governments is the setting for this executive: it would implement decisions made within an agreed world system. Its functions are distinct from those of the legislature and tribunal, which make laws and adjudicate disputes, respectively.
