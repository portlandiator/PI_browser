# How the Bahá'í Writings are translated: practice and guidance

Researched 2026-11-05. Quotations come from the primary texts as fetched (bahai.org PDFs, or Research Department compilations reposted on Bahá'í Library Online). Anything marked **unverified** could not be confirmed from a source.

Key sources:
- [LS] Research Department, *Literary Style—Translation*: https://bahai-library.com/pdf/compilations/compilation_literary_style_translation.pdf
- [IND] Research Department, *Guidance on Translating the Bahá'í Writings into Indigenous and Other Languages*: https://bahai-library.com/pdf/compilations/compilation_translating_writings_indigenous.pdf
- [P99] *Policies Governing Authorized and Provisional Translations…*, 4 July 1999: https://www.bahai.org/library/authoritative-texts/the-universal-house-of-justice/messages/19990704_001/19990704_001.pdf

## 1. Shoghi Effendi's method and principles

- **Faithful and beautiful at once.** ‘Abdu’l-Bahá: translations should be made "conformably to the originals, and with power and grace of style… Do thy very best to ensure that the translation is in conformity with the original." [LS]
- **The King James register was a deliberate choice.** "Shoghi Effendi himself uses the King James version of the Bible, both because it is an authoritative one and in beautiful English" (28 Oct 1949) [LS]. The House of Justice describes his style as "very beautiful and poetic… using many words which might be considered archaic and are reminiscent of the English used by the translators of the King James version" (29 Oct 1973) [LS]. The Research Department calls the slightly archaic style "a bridge between the English of today and the style of the Persian and Arabic Writings" (1 Jul 1985) [LS].
- **He did not translate word for word.** "A literal translation is often a bad one… a translator is at times compelled to convey the meaning of the original by means of a form of words suited to the language" (29 Oct 1973) [LS].
- **Context decides the rendering, not a fixed one-to-one term list.** Translators "frequently have to use various different synonyms in English… of the same Arabic or Persian word… Conversely, they may have to use the same English word… to translate various different words" (31 May 1981) [LS]. (A further quotation, that a "rigid list" of equivalents would be "definitely misleading", could not be found in the cited compilation and is **unverified**.) For example, "Bahá" is "Glory" in *Bahá'u'lláh* but "Splendour" in the month names [IND]. Moojan Momen's study of how he rendered law-related terms looks at the same question: https://mail.bahai-library.com/ris/4579
- **Surface conventions stay consistent.** Choose "he does" or "he doeth" and never mix them. Drop "His Holiness" before Prophets' names. Pronouns for the Manifestation or the Master "should… invariably be capitalized" (8 Nov 1948) [LS].
- **Choosing words with care.** He checked words in "Webster's big dictionary" and often chose "the third or fourth usage… sometimes bordering on the archaic". He "would never explain or comment on a text submitted to him in English (when it was not his own translation) until he had verified it with the original" (Rúhíyyih Rabbani, *The Priceless Pearl*, quoted in [LS]).
- **His translations are interpretive, and he revised them.** "The beloved Guardian was not only a translator but the inspired Interpreter" (8 Sep 1964, quoted 16 Sep 1992): https://bahai-library.com/uhj_english_translation. Yet "Shoghi Effendi does not consider even his own translations as final" (14 Aug 1930): https://bahai-library.com/pdf/compilations/translation.pdf. He revised *The Hidden Words* and republished it (Brookshaw): https://www.bahai-library.com/bsr_1-10_files/bsr09/9D1_commentaries.htm
- **Transliteration.** On 12 March 1923 he introduced a standard system for oriental terms, calling it an "authoritative and universal, though arbitrary code" (26 Nov 1923, via Marzieh Gail's *Bahá'í Glossary*): https://www.bahai-library.com/glossary/remarks.html. It is largely based on the 10th International Congress of Orientalists (Geneva, 1894), with digraphs such as "sh" and assimilated sun letters (Momen): https://www.bahai-library.com/momen_transliteration. The World Centre published an overview with a list of common terms in 2023: https://bahai-library.com/bwc_transliteration_system_bahai and https://www.bahai.org/library/transliteration/. Familiar place names such as Teheran and Shiraz need not be transliterated in materials for the general public (12 Aug 1998): https://www.bahai.org/library/authoritative-texts/the-universal-house-of-justice/messages/19980812_001/19980812_001.pdf
- **Notes and glossaries.** He wanted the Aqdas translation "copiously annotated". Its notes target points unclear to non-Arabic readers (Kitáb-i-Aqdas, Preface/Introduction): https://www.bahai.org/library/authoritative-texts/bahaullah/kitab-i-aqdas/2. That he compiled glossaries for his own translations is **unverified**.

## 2. How authorized translations are produced today

- **Who does it.** "Tablets… translated into English for use or publication by the Bahá'í World Centre will continue to be reviewed by an ad hoc translation and review committee at the World Center and designated as authorized translations" [P99]. An "authorized" translation is "one approved by one or more translation committees appointed by the House of Justice" (Research Department memo, 1994, as summarised at https://bahai-library.com/uhj_provisional_translations). No public description of a named "translation department" or of its internal workflow was found (**unverified**).
- **Committees, not individuals.** "‘Abdu’l-Bahá has explained that, ideally, all translations of the Sacred Texts should be made by competent committees, rather than by individuals" (8 Sep 1985) [IND].
- **The Aqdas model (1992).** The work was "of the utmost difficulty, requiring consultation with experts in many lands". About one third had already been translated by Shoghi Effendi. The rest followed three aims: "accuracy of meaning, beauty of English, and conformity of style with that used by Shoghi Effendi". Committees prepared and reviewed the translation and wrote the annotations: https://www.bahai.org/library/authoritative-texts/bahaullah/kitab-i-aqdas/3
- **The pattern in later volumes:**
  - *Summons of the Lord of Hosts* (2002): "Wherever possible, translations made by Shoghi Effendi have been incorporated… approximately one third." The rest was done by "committees and individuals appointed", "faithful to the meaning… and consistent with the exalted English style established by the Guardian". The translators "benefited from consulting" E. G. Browne's earlier translation. The volume ends with a "Key to Passages Translated by Shoghi Effendi": https://www.bahai.org/library/authoritative-texts/bahaullah/summons-lord-hosts/summons-lord-hosts.pdf
  - *Days of Remembrance* (2017): "Eight of the selections were previously translated by Shoghi Effendi". A table of his passages is at the end, and the rest are mostly first English translations: https://www.bahai.org/library/authoritative-texts/bahaullah/days-remembrance/2 and https://news.bahai.org/story/1148
  - *The Call of the Divine Beloved* (2019): the Seven Valleys and Four Valleys "are based on the translations by Marzieh Gail, in consultation with Ali-Kuli Khan… some changes were required for clarity and accuracy": https://www.bahai.org/library/authoritative-texts/bahaullah/call-divine-beloved/2
  - *Light of the World* (2021): "seventy-six newly translated selections" from volumes 3–4 of *Muntakhabátí az Makátíb*. No translator is named: https://www.bahai.org/library/authoritative-texts/abdul-baha/light-of-the-world/2
- **Online release.** Since 2018 the Reference Library has published individual Tablets, in English translation with the Persian/Arabic original, "when they become available". This runs alongside work to catalogue the texts for publication in the original languages and English: https://news.bahai.org/story/1283
- **Approval is required.** English translations and revisions "must be checked at the World Centre and officially approved before publication" (8 Sep 1991): https://bahai-library.com/uhj_translation_review

## 3. Provisional translations by individuals

- "Individual believers are free to translate into English any of the Holy Writings for their own use. Such translations may also be issued electronically, and may be published in circulars or journals without review by anyone other than the editors… but they must, in all cases, be clearly labeled as 'provisional translations.'" [P99]
- **Books and pamphlets.** The manuscript goes to the World Centre for clearance "together with copies of the Arabic or Persian texts used". If its quality is adequate it is returned with a note, and work below standard is "returned to the translator for reworking". The normal review by the National Spiritual Assembly still applies [P99]. The World Centre judges "the quality of the English rendering" and whether there are "any major doubtful points with regard to the accuracy" (30 Jun 1999): https://www.bahai.org/library/authoritative-texts/the-universal-house-of-justice/messages/19990630_001/1
- **Where a Guardian translation exists, use it.** "The beloved Guardian's translations are obviously the most authentic and should be used". Using a different translation is a matter to refer to the House of Justice (3 Nov 1996): https://bahai-library.com/pdf/compilations/translation.pdf
- Provisional translations "are not likely to be used as a basis for translations into other languages" (8 Sep 1991): https://bahai-library.com/uhj_translation_review
- A Tablet should not appear in translation for the first time as a footnote or appendix to a historical document [P99, Appendix III].

## 4. Translating into other languages

- **Work from the Guardian's English.** "With the exception of certain oriental languages such as Turkish, Arabic and Urdu… new translations of the Sacred Text into languages other than English must be made from the Guardian's English translation where it exists. When there is no translation into English by Shoghi Effendi… the National Spiritual Assembly… should seek the advice of the Universal House of Justice" (28 Mar 1971) [IND].
- **Check against the originals.** The Dutch committee was to translate "from the English translations rather than from the original Persian or Arabic", consulting Persian speakers "when the English wording seems ambiguous" (31 May 1981) [IND]. French translators should involve "Bahá'ís who are fluent in French and are also familiar with the original Arabic or Persian" (2 Dec 1988) [IND]. Translators "should also check their translation with the original texts" (16 Sep 1992): https://bahai-library.com/uhj_english_translation
- **Interim routes.** Georgian: "In principle, the desirable course is to translate from English". Translating from Russian is acceptable as a provisional measure (23 May 1995) [IND].
- **Following the target language's rules.** Keep "as much uniformity with the English translations as possible", but no "flagrant violation of the rules of the language". For example, follow French practice on capitalizing pronouns for God (15 Feb 1957) [LS]. Long sentences by Shoghi Effendi "must be divided into shorter ones" in French (2 Dec 1988) [IND].
- **Keeping the elevated register.** "The style in the original is an exalted one and this aspect should not be lost" (12 Aug 1973). Everyday language "should not be an overriding consideration" (7 Oct 1973) [IND].
- **Simplification.** It is permitted to help translation into languages with small vocabularies, but "it is not permissible to publish simplifications and paraphrases… as Bahá'í Scripture" (13 Mar 1969) [IND].
- **Regional authorities.** Example: the International Panel for Spanish Translations (1993). It produces "authorized Spanish versions" and is kept informed of "corrections and revisions to the authorized English versions". All Spanish translations of authoritative texts need its approval, and it keeps "copies of new translations as well as corrections". Quotations in secondary works "should conform to the current authorized versions": https://bahai-library.com/pdf/uhj/uhj_translation_writings_spanish.pdf
- **No published style sheet.** No comprehensive translation style sheet or guideline document was found. The Research Department said in 1999 that "there is, at present, no one compilation that covers all aspects of the translation process": https://bahai-library.com/pdf/compilations/translation.pdf. Any internal World Centre style guide is **unverified**.

## 5. Tools and reference works

- **Gail, *Bahá'í Glossary*.** Pronunciation and meaning of Persian and Arabic terms, built on Shoghi Effendi's transliteration: https://www.bahai-library.com/glossary/remarks.html
- **Eshraghian, *A Collection of Selected Words from the Bahá'í Writings in Persian, Arabic, and English*** (1989; book 2000). Over 2,200 entries, built by "matching the words in the original Persian / Arabic text with the same word or phrase in the published English translation", so it captures Shoghi Effendi's actual renderings rather than dictionary meanings: https://bahai-library.com/6543
- **The World Centre transliteration list (2023)**: https://www.bahai.org/library/transliteration/
- **Bahá'í Reference Library.** Authoritative texts, with Persian/Arabic originals released alongside translations, and search: https://news.bahai.org/story/1283 and https://www.bahai.org/library/. Whether its search covers Persian/Arabic full text is **unverified**.
- **Ocean.** Chad Jones's multi-religion search library, started in 1998: https://bahai-library.com/jones_ocean. Coverage of the originals is **unverified**.
- **Earlier translations as aids.** Browne's translation was used for the Lawḥ-i-Sulṭán. Gail and Khan's translations were the basis for the Valleys (see §2).
- **The Qur'án.** Study of the Qur'án is "indispensable" for understanding Bahá'u'lláh's writings. Sale's translation is "the most scholarly" and Rodwell's "more literary" (23 Nov 1934) [LS].
- **Bilingual dictionaries.** Use of Steingass, Lane's Lexicon or Ḥaïm by World Centre translators is **unverified**. They are widely assumed but no source was found.

## 6. Known debates and difficulties

- **Archaic register.**
  - Lewis and Brookshaw argue that dropping "thou"/"ye" is "both feasible and appropriate at the start of the 21st century". Brookshaw adds that the style must stay compatible with the original's aesthetics: https://www.bahai-library.com/bsr_1-10_files/bsr09/9D1_commentaries.htm
  - The House of Justice rejects simplified "dilutions": "the standard of presentation of the divine Word cannot be lowered". Instead, "it is possible… to accompany the Texts with explanatory material" (12 Aug 1998): https://www.bahai.org/library/authoritative-texts/the-universal-house-of-justice/messages/19980812_001/19980812_001.pdf
  - Changing familiar scriptural wording too often "can be gravely disturbing" (3 Feb 1988, cited by Brookshaw).
- **Three source languages and many styles.** The originals are "in three different languages (Arabic, Persian and a few in Turkish)… in a number of different styles. Some are highly poetic and metaphorical, others more precise" (1 Jul 1985; 27 May 1987) [LS]. Arabic's terseness forces the translator to add implicit words without "limiting the meaning", and legal terms differ between Arabic and English (Aqdas Introduction, link in §2).
- **Symbols have no fixed meanings.** "It would not be possible… to compile a list of meanings for specific symbols, expressions and words, since they may vary in their implication from passage to passage" (8 Sep 1985) [IND].
- **Inclusive language.** "We do not feel at liberty to change Shoghi Effendi's usage" (27 Nov 1989). The Writings "do not use the 'gender inclusive language' now in vogue" (26 Jul 1996): https://bahai-library.com/compilation_masculine_gender_writings
- **Taste.** "Translation is a very difficult art—an art in which absolute perfection is unattainable" (20 Sep 1982) [LS].
- **Several defensible renderings.** Malouf's comparison of four *Hidden Words* translations shows that "several distinct renderings are often possible" (via Brookshaw, above). See also Malouf, *Unveiling the Hidden Words* (George Ronald, 1997): https://bahai-library.com/86

## Recommended model for the tool

- **Use prior authorized renderings first.** For any passage, first surface Shoghi Effendi's translation, then other authorized World Centre translations, as the reference renderings. This matches the "wherever possible… incorporated" practice and the "Key to Passages Translated by Shoghi Effendi".
- **Build the term base from aligned text, not dictionaries.** Follow Eshraghian's method: align original words to the authorized English. Show every attested rendering of a word with its context and frequency, not a single fixed equivalent, in line with the verified 1981 guidance that one word may need different English renderings in different contexts.
- **Label translations by status, everywhere.** Mark each text as Guardian, authorized (World Centre) or provisional (with translator name). Provisional text must always carry "provisional translation" and never be presented as authorized. Draft suggestions from the tool are provisional by definition.
- **Keep the original beside the translation.** Show the Persian/Arabic source with every translation, and keep a pointer to the exact source text used. The World Centre requires those texts with any provisional submission.
- **Lint the house style.** Check transliteration against the official 2023 list (with an option for common spellings in public materials), capitalization of pronouns for the Manifestation, and consistent verb forms (no mixing of "doeth" and "does").
- **Help readers with notes and glossaries, not simplification.** Offer glosses, dictionary pop-ups, notes and paraphrase-for-study clearly separated from the text. The House of Justice permits "explanatory material" but not simplified scripture.
- **For other target languages,** default to the authorized English as the base and show the original side by side, to resolve ambiguity as the Dutch and French guidance describes.
- **Track versions.** Record which edition or revision a rendering comes from. Guardian translations were themselves revised, and regional panels track "corrections and revisions".
- **Support review, not just drafting.** Provide a committee-style review step (accuracy vs original, quality of English, consistency with Shoghi Effendi's style) and export a submission package (translation plus source text) for the World Centre route described in the 1999 policy.
